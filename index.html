StepHaven

Privacy Policy

Effective 21 October 2026

The short version

  • Your step count (and wheelchair pushes, or the workout data of an Ember you start) is used on your phone. It is never sent to us, to analytics or to anyone else, and it is never stored in StepHaven's iCloud data.
  • There are no accounts. Your havens are stored on your phone and in your own iCloud, which we can't see.
  • StepHaven has no ads, does no tracking, and never sells or shares your data for advertising.
  • We only receive what's described below: anonymous usage counts, the join codes, join requests and reports that shared havens need, and bug reports you choose to send.

StepHaven is a city-building game for iPhone: the steps you walk become points you spend on a town. This policy explains what information the app uses, where it goes, how long it's kept, and the choices you have. StepHaven is made by Brody R., an independent developer in the United States, who is responsible for the information described here. "We" and "us" in this policy mean the developer.

Steps and Apple Health

If you allow it, StepHaven reads your step count from Apple Health to work out how many points you've earned. If you use a wheelchair and turn on Wheelchair Pushes, it also reads the wheelchair pushes your Apple Watch records, which earn points the same way. When you light an Ember before a workout, it also reads the active calories (Active Energy) and exercise minutes your Apple Watch records during that Ember only, so workouts like cycling or swimming can earn points too. It reads only those four kinds of data, it reads workout data only during the Embers you start, and it never writes to Health.

  • Your step, push and workout data is used on your phone to calculate points, levels, boosters and rewards (for example, how long you've been walking toward a merchant's visit or a hatching egg). It is never sent to us, to analytics or to anyone else, never stored in StepHaven's iCloud data, and never used for advertising.
  • To keep things fair, StepHaven keeps a record of each day's counted movement on your phone once the day has settled, so steps added to Health later don't change it. From StepHaven's second build on, this record is kept out of your iPhone's own backup. A few of the app's settings and totals stored on your phone can be part of that backup, which StepHaven can't read.
  • Whether you count pushes is a setting kept on your phone. The Embers you light (when and for how long) are kept on your phone for the app, its widgets and its Live Activity. Neither is backed up to StepHaven's iCloud data or sent anywhere.
  • StepHaven's widgets and Live Activity show your points, today's steps, your goal and a running Ember on your Home Screen and Lock Screen. That information is shared only between the app and its widgets on your phone.
  • You can turn Health access off at any time in the Settings app, under Health → Data Access & Devices → StepHaven. The game still works without it; you just won't earn points from walking, pushing or workouts.

Your havens and progress

What you build, your inventory, levels and similar game progress are saved on your phone. If you're signed in to iCloud, StepHaven also backs them up to your own private iCloud storage through Apple's CloudKit, so you can restore them on a new phone. StepHaven's developer cannot see the contents of your private iCloud data.

The backup holds your game progress, such as your havens and their names, items, points spent, your experience total (which grows as you walk and play, but isn't a step count), the name and colour you chose for shared havens, and the players you've hidden or blocked. It never holds your step, push or calorie counts. After a restore, anything that depends on your walking is worked out again from Apple Health on your phone.

Photos you take in photo mode, and pictures of your havens shown in the app, are kept in the app on your phone. They aren't backed up to StepHaven's iCloud data or sent anywhere unless you share them.

Shared havens

If you start or join a shared haven, its contents are stored in the iCloud of the person who started it and shared with everyone who joins:

  • Everyone in a shared haven can see and change what's built there, including the names of Workshop buildings and pets. Each item records which player placed it, so an item someone else removes can be returned to the player who placed it. Players are identified by a random number created when the app is installed, never by email or Apple Account.
  • You can choose a name and a colour to show your friends (in the app's Settings). It's optional and doesn't need to be your real name; if you don't choose one, the app uses "Player" and a short code. Your name and colour are stored with each shared haven you're in, where everyone in it can see them, and with your iCloud backup. Names that friends can see (player, haven, pet and building names) are checked on your phone against a word filter.
  • Each shared haven keeps a list of who is in it, by the same random number, with the dates each player joined and left, the name and colour each player chose, the haven's progress level when each player joined (so joining a haven someone else built up doesn't count as your own progress), and an anonymous iCloud identifier that Apple gives StepHaven for each player, so the person who started the haven can remove or block someone. This identifier is not your Apple Account, email or name.
  • The haven's name, its picture and look, and the time zone of the person who started it (so everyone sees the same time of day) are visible to everyone in the haven. When a friend builds there, your phone may show a notification with their name and the haven's name.

Some of what shared havens need is kept in Apple's public CloudKit database, which, unlike your private iCloud data, StepHaven's developer can read:

  • Join codes. Joining uses a six-character code, stored with the haven's name and the link needed to ask to join. Only share the code with people you trust. It is deleted once everyone has left the haven.
  • Join requests. A request to join holds the name and colour you chose and your anonymous iCloud identifier, so the haven's creator can add you. Anyone who has the same join code could see it. The app deletes it once you're let in or turned down, when you withdraw it, or after seven days. If the app is removed before then, we delete leftover requests ourselves.
  • Declines and blocks. If the creator turns down or blocks a request, a small record holding the join code and the requester's anonymous iCloud identifier is kept so they aren't asked again. It's deleted if the creator unblocks them.
  • Reports. If you report another player (Report a Member), the report holds the reason you pick, anything you write, that player's name, random number and anonymous iCloud identifier, the haven's name and identifier, and your own anonymous iCloud identifier. Only you and StepHaven's moderators can read it, and the player you report isn't told. If a report can't be sent, the app offers to send it by email instead.

Nobody can delete a shared haven for everyone from inside the app, because friends may still be building there. When you leave one, it's removed from your phone at once, and what you built stays in the haven for your friends. Once every player has left, the haven and its join code are deleted from iCloud. If the person who started a haven deletes StepHaven's data from their iCloud (see Deleting your data), that haven is deleted for everyone.

Reporting a bug

If you send a bug report from the app (Settings → Help → Report a Bug), it's stored in Apple's public CloudKit database, readable only by you and StepHaven's developer. It holds the category you pick and what you write, the app's version and build, your iPhone model, iOS version and the app's language, how many havens you have and whether you're in a shared haven, a picture of your haven's map if you choose to add one, and your anonymous iCloud identifier. It never includes step counts. If it can't be sent, the app offers to send it by email instead.

Emailing us

If you email us, or send a report or bug report by email, we receive your email address and whatever the email contains (a report sent by email includes the player and haven details listed above). We use it only to answer you and look into the problem.

Anonymous analytics

StepHaven uses TelemetryDeck, based in Germany, to count how the app is used, for example how many people finish the introduction, open the Workshop, or view the StepHaven+ screen. TelemetryDeck is designed for privacy: it doesn't collect personal data, and the identifier it uses is a one-way hash created on your device, so it can't be linked back to you.

  • Along with each count, TelemetryDeck records general information about the app and device: the app version, iPhone model, iOS version, screen size, language, region and time zone, light or dark mode, and how often the app is used (such as the number of sessions and days it's been opened). StepHaven's first build (version 1.0, build 1) also sent some display and accessibility settings, such as text size, bold text and Reduce Motion; later builds don't.
  • Analytics never include health data (no step, push or calorie counts, not even rough ranges, and nothing about whether you've connected Apple Health or count pushes) or anything you've typed, such as names or join codes. For Embers, analytics count only that one was lit, its chosen length, where it was lit from (such as the app, a widget or Siri) and whether it earned any points, never how many points or any calorie or minute figures.
  • The welcome screens may ask where you heard about StepHaven. Answering is optional; if you pick an answer (such as TikTok or a friend), only that choice is counted.
  • When you buy something, the analytics count the purchase: which product, whether it was a free trial, the App Store country, the currency and the price. They don't include anything that identifies you or your Apple Account.
  • The data is used only to improve the game. It is not used for advertising and is not combined with data from other apps or websites.

Measuring Apple Ads

StepHaven may be advertised with Apple Ads. To tell whether those ads work, the first time you open the app it asks Apple's AdServices framework whether StepHaven was installed from an Apple Ads ad. If it was, Apple returns which campaign, ad group, keyword and ad led to the download, the type of download and the App Store country, and the app counts that once in the anonymous analytics above. If it wasn't, nothing is counted. This uses no advertising identifier (IDFA), doesn't track you across other apps or websites, and the app never asks for tracking permission. iOS may also tell Apple's ad networks (SKAdNetwork) that the app was installed, which Apple does without identifying you.

Crash and performance reports

iOS can give apps reports about crashes, freezes and slow launches through Apple's MetricKit. StepHaven keeps the most recent reports on your phone and sends only the type of problem (for example, "a crash" and its technical cause) through the anonymous analytics above, so problems can be found and fixed. The reports contain technical information about the app, not personal information.

If you've chosen to share analytics with app developers in your iPhone's settings, Apple may also share crash information with the developer. That is handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy.

Purchases

StepHaven+ and item packs are sold through the App Store. Apple handles payment; StepHaven never sees your card or payment details. The app learns which StepHaven+ plan or packs your Apple Account has, so it can unlock what you've paid for, and counts each purchase in the anonymous analytics described above. To give early players a founder's gift, the app also checks, on your phone, the date the App Store says you first downloaded StepHaven; that date isn't sent anywhere. You manage subscriptions in the Settings app, under your name → Subscriptions.

Photos

Photo mode takes a picture of your haven. Each one is kept in the app's album on your phone; it isn't backed up or sent anywhere. It is only saved to your library or shared when you choose to, through the iOS share sheet. StepHaven never reads your existing photos.

What StepHaven doesn't do

  • No accounts, no sign-up, and no email address or phone number asked for (we only see your email address if you email us).
  • No location data and no contacts.
  • No ads in the game, no advertising identifiers, and no tracking across other apps or websites.
  • No selling, renting or trading of any data, and no sharing of it for advertising.

Who handles your data

  • Apple provides Apple Health, iCloud and CloudKit (where havens, backups, join codes, requests and reports are stored), the App Store, Apple Ads measurement and MetricKit, under Apple's privacy policy. Apple may store this data outside your country.
  • TelemetryDeck provides the anonymous analytics, under its privacy policy.

How long it's kept

  • Your havens, progress and backup: until you delete them (see below).
  • Join codes: until everyone has left the haven. Join requests: up to seven days. Decline and block records: until the player is unblocked.
  • Reports and bug reports: until they're dealt with, and no longer than a year.
  • Emails: as long as needed to help you.
  • Analytics: kept by TelemetryDeck in a form that can't be linked to you.

Deleting your data

  • Delete a haven from the Havens screen in the app. It's removed from your phone at once and from your iCloud backup as soon as you're online, and the pieces you'd placed go back to your inventory. A shared haven is removed from iCloud once every player has left (see Shared havens).
  • Delete all of StepHaven's iCloud data in the Settings app: your name → iCloud → Storage → StepHaven. This also deletes any shared havens you started, for everyone in them.
  • Withdraw a join request from the Havens screen. To have a report, bug report or anything else in the public database removed, email us.
  • Deleting the app removes everything stored on your phone, including its widgets' data.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example in the EU, the UK or California), you may have the right to ask what information we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, to object to how it's used, and to complain to your data protection authority. Because we don't have accounts, most of your information is only on your phone and in your own iCloud, where you control it directly. For anything we hold, such as reports, bug reports or emails, email us and we'll help. Where the law requires a reason for using information, ours are: providing the game you asked for (saving, sharing and restoring havens), keeping shared havens safe (join requests, blocks and reports), and our legitimate interest in understanding and improving the game (anonymous analytics, crash reports and ad measurement).

Children

StepHaven is for a general audience and doesn't ask anyone for personal information. Shared havens are invite-only and have no chat, and names other players can see are filtered. If you're a parent and think your child has sent us something personal, such as in a report or an email, contact us and we'll delete it.
